Four findings, consistent across every codebase and cross-checked by the definition-plane probes. (1) Holistic cold-strict compressibility (102–2131×) is set by the codebase, not its size — and for the own-code-heavy set no measured method reaches 400× (a measured upper bound + open question, not a proof). (2) The S0 semantic-Root memo makes the repeated-build basis (the production reality: a persistent per-daemon dictionary) clear 400× on every codebase, 3706–41102×. (3) Throughput is airtight: a real two-process socketpair sustains ≥1 GB/s single-stream, byte-exact. (4) Source-conditioning the definition plane (toolchain headers free, since icecream ships the environment) lifts a codebase's dict-leg ratio by exactly its toolchain fraction — 2–4× for toolchain-heavy libraries, only ~1.1× for own-code-dominated apps — so it rescues most sub-400× libraries but cannot rescue DuckDB (269×). Cold-400× on a single build is a property of how much unique code a codebase ships.

2   Holistic cold FinalRatio — the z≤3 production number and its z19+LDM ceiling

Log scale. Bars are the production number (z≤3, the owner's hard cap); the ◆ diamonds are a diagnostic ceiling that violates the cap — the whole codec re-compressed at z19 + long-distance-matching + 27-bit window (same six wire categories, batched, region leg min(delta,raw)). The gap is the headroom the z≤3 cap leaves, and it splits the 15 codebases three ways against 400×: ships at z3 (Eigen, Catch2, range-v3, OpenCV — already ≥400× under the cap); cap-limited (cereal, abseil, LLVM — z3<400 but the ceiling clears it, so the cap is what holds them under, not the codebase); and codebase-limited (the other 8 — still <400× even at the z19+LDM ceiling, DuckDB 213× the hardest).

Honest reading of the codebase-limited set: no method we measured — z3..z19, LDM, front-code, skeleton-columnar, PPM/MTF, cross-project pretraining, trained dicts, source-conditioned COPY-programs — reaches a holistic 400× on them. But a compressed size is a description-length upper bound, not a theorem: cold-400× on the own-code-heavy codebases is an open question (a codebase-aware model could still get there — e.g. an untested program-transformation coder projects ~414× on DuckDB), not proven impossible.

3   S0 amortized (40 builds) — ≥400x everywhere

Repeated-build basis: build 0 ships the dictionary cold; unchanged TUs in later builds are a 56-byte ROOT_REF. Every codebase clears 400× by 9–100× — this is where the win lives.

4   Source-conditioning: the lift equals the toolchain fraction

The definition/dict plane, plain (grey) vs source-conditioned (toolchain headers free, since icecream ships the environment). The gap is the lift — and it is proportional to how much of a codebase's .ii is toolchain: libraries (toolchain-dominated) get a 2–4× lift that carries the sub-400× ones (spdlog/nlohmann-json/re2/LevelDB) over the line; apps (own-code-dominated) get only ~1.1×. So source-conditioning rescues 4 of the 6 dict-leg corpora that were under 400× plain, but can't rescue DuckDB (lowest base ratio AND smallest toolchain fraction → 269×), with fmt right on the line. This is the dict leg only; the holistic cold (panel 2) is additionally bounded by the per-TU structure legs.

(Own-source fraction alone is NOT monotonic with ratio: Eigen is 65% own-source yet tops the chart at 5066×, because its own header-only templates are reused across all 650 TUs — huge within-build self-repetition. Header-only-template libraries are the exception; per-corpus own-source fractions are in the table.)

5   Cross-project coverage saturates fast — and low for apps

defcodec's leave-one-out learning curves: each codebase's line coverage from a prior built of the k largest OTHER projects. Apps plateau near 5% (own code is unique — a bigger prior barely helps); libraries saturate at 40–75% (mostly shared system headers). Curves flatten by k≈3–9: cross-project transfer is real but bounded and quickly exhausted.

Seed vs self-bootstrap

A pretrained cross-project seed and a from-scratch (empty) online codec, run over the same first 200 translation units of 25 C++ projects. The question: how much does the seed actually transfer — and does it survive a toolchain change? Metric is wire bytes per input byte per TU (log scale); lower is better.

What it means

Most of the compression is project-local self-bootstrap. The empty codec alone reaches high ratios (DuckDB 391×, Firefox 767×, Godot 689×) purely by learning each project's own recurring structure across its first 200 TUs. The seed is a modest, front-loaded top-up — a median +17% when the toolchain matches, biggest in the early TUs and fading as the project catches up.

And it does not cross a toolchain boundary. The three clang/libc++ projects (Firefox, ClickHouse, V8) show a seed contribution of essentially zero against the gcc-11 seed — because preprocessed .ii is toolchain-specific down to the bytes. This is the empirical case for what local-oracle's report proposes: the shipped universal bootstrap must be trained from raw source and parameterized structural programs (toolchain-invariant), with the toolchain byte-substrate derived per-env from the shipped toolchain — not an .ii seed, which is only a mechanism-and-accounting control.
